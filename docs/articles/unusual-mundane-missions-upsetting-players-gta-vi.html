Short answer

Yeah, the missions making you do groceries, fix sinks and buy flowers are real. It's not a bug. But they're mostly optional and part of Chapter 1’s slower build-up. Rockstar’s trying for emotional depth, but some folks expected chaos sooner.

What’s actually confirmed

Rockstar wanted us to feel the push‑pull in Jason and Lucia’s life—not just bank‑rob crime spree right away. The writer said these low‑stakes tasks are meant to sync your rhythm with theirs. And Rockstar warned it’s not about grinding nonstop, so expect some domestic drudgery early on.

Examples: you’ll pick up groceries, buy flowers, maybe even do small chores around their place before Lucia gets out of jail. Tons of players noticed this—stairs down from “hot launch frenzy” to everyday errands. One outlet says players get wrapped up doing everything from working out to trash runs and apartment renos.

On top of that, Chapter 1 lasts 30–40 minutes before you get full sandbox freedom, and it leans into that domestic vibe.

Why some folks don't dig it

Some streamers and commenters roasted it as “Good Boyfriend Simulator” or “too woke.” Asmongold freaked out when asked to unload groceries from the trunk. Another called it “humiliating” and said Jason looks like a clown––major cringe.

There’s also talk of it being a “girlfriend simulator” that’s grinding rather than wild action—especially when you're fresh into a GTA expecting mayhem.

But it smooths out later

A former Rockstar lead—who’s about 40‑odd percent into the story—says Chapter 2 opens up way more. He calls it the point where GTA “magic kicks in.” So yeah, stick it out. That early stuff is groundwork.

My two cents

I get the gripes. We all came here to blow stuff up, not water plants. But that bread‑and‑butter drip sets tone. If you hate it, you can ignore most of it. And once the world smashes open, none of that will matter.

FAQ

Most of them are optional props and story beats in Chapter 1. Rockstar built them to pace the narrative, not stall you.

Yep. A Rockstar lead playing forward says Chapter 2 introduces the sandbox the series is known for.
