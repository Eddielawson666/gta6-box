Fixing washed‑out HDR in GTA VI

Short answer: yes, there’s a fix—but it’s a bit of dancing between your TV, console and the game’s wonky HDR sliders. You’ll need to run things in the right order and maybe use some workaround numbers until Rockstar irons it out.

What’s actually broken

HDTVTest found that GTA VI ignores your system‑wide HDR calibration. The in‑game sliders don’t match what ends up on your screen—peak brightness underperforms, midtones blow out, blacks look grey, highlights lose contrast. Rockstar hasn’t said a word about fixing it yet.

So if you followed the normal setup (adjust until logo barely visible), you’re probably looking at washed‑out, flat visuals.

Needed first: fix your TV

  • Enable Game Mode on your TV.
  • Turn on HGiG in your TV’s HDR menu—or turn off any extra “Dynamic Tone Mapping”. Without that, your TV remaps the image again and messes everything up.
  • This step alone can fix that grey, flat look for most people.

Then your console

Run your console’s HDR setup (like PS5’s in Settings → Video Output or Xbox’s Calibrate HDR), but don’t count on it taking. GTA VI ignores it. Still, go through it just to cover your base.

Now in‑game calibration (only when HDR’s on)

Go to Pause → Settings → Display → HDR Calibration. Ignore what the sliders say–use this as a visual test, not a number trap.

HDTVTest’s baseline for a 1,000‑nit capable screen:

SettingBaseline Value
Max Brightness2000

Remember: these are a starting point—not magic numbers. Tweak to taste, room lighting, screen type.

Captures looking off?

If your screenshots or clips look dull or bizarre compared to actual gameplay, that’s HDR capture acting up. Fix: turn off HDR before you take screenshots or record, then toggle it back on afterward.

On PC

Rockstar hasn’t confirmed any PC version yet, so no official HDR tweaks to reference. When—if—they do launch it, assume you’ll still need to override in‑game sliders or wait for mods or developers to patch in better calibration.

Wrap‑up

Fix order: TV (Game Mode + HGiG) → Console HDR setup → In‑game HDR calibration using visuals not numbers. Use the HDTVTest baseline if you want a head‑start, then tweak. Capture woes? Turn off HDR first. No PC info yet, so we wait.

FAQ

Why does GTA VI look washed out even if I set HDR correctly on my console?

Because GTA VI ignores your console’s HDR calibration and double‑maps HDR if your TV also applies tone‑mapping. You need to disable TV dynamic tone mapping (or enable HGiG), then run its calibration, then use the in‑game HDR sliders.

Can I just set the sliders to exact numbers and call it a day?

You can use 2000 / 20 / 35 as a starting point on 1,000‑nit displays, but those numbers are a baseline, not a law. Your room, screen type, and lighting matter. Always fine‑tune by eye.

Why do my screenshots look different from gameplay?

HDR captures often get tone‑mapped incorrectly outside the game. Solution: turn HDR off before grabbing a screenshot or clip, and switch it back on to play.

On PC, is there a fix yet?

Rockstar hasn’t confirmed a PC version or HDR calibration tools. Until then, treat any numbers you find online as speculative.
