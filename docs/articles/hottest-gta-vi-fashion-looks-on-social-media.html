The short answer

You won’t find an official runway in GTA VI, but social media’s lit up with players dressing Jason and Lucia in a wild range of styles. Some of that is for real—Rockstar let you switch outfits, hit salons, and stash clothes in your trunk. The rest? Mostly fan-made creativity riding on a hype wave.

What’s actually in the game right now

  • You can switch your characters’ looks—hairstyles, makeup, outfits—at barbers, beauty salons, and fitting rooms. Clothes even go in your trunk so you can change on the fly.
  • Changing your look actually matters—helps you ditch police clues.
  • The Vintage Vice City Pack is included with pre-orders and brings a retro party vibe: think red backless dresses, grey jackets with rolled-up sleeves, that neon-drenched nightlife energy.

So yeah—Rockstar gave us the tools. Didn’t invent outfits for your feed—but gave you enough canvas to make your own.

What’s blowing up online

Across X (fka Twitter), TikTok, Instagram: people are styling Lucia and Jason like they’re on a fashion shoot rather than a crime spree. Lucias sporting pop-punk crop tops, headscarves, high-fashion festival looks. One user dropped a set of lavender pants, leather mini and jacket mix—millions of views. Others tossed them in sleek, studio-lit poses that look straight from a fragrance ad. It’s all fan styling, but it looks stunning and is being shared like crazy.

Launch parties turned fashion events

Then there’s real life. The GTA VI launch in Miami had Alex Consani showing up in seat‑belt‑strap dress, sheer tops, lace leggings and brocade boots. Totally GTA‑inspired, totally Vice City glam, totally not from the game itself—but definitely echoing the vibe people are going for in the game world.

So what’s confirmed, and what’s not?

  • Confirmed: you get outfit variety, hair and makeup swaps, clothes affect the world (like police heat), the Vintage Vice City Pack gives some official style cues.
  • Not confirmed: any specific celeb wardrobe list. All those posts on TikTok or galleries? They’re players styling what the game let them—and creators leaning into fandom, not Rockstar’s fashion catalog.

Why it matters

GTA’s always been about chaos. Now it’s about expression. That ability to dress up your characters and hit photo mode is giving players and fans a way to do theatre, to share glimpses of themselves through Lucia and Jason. The characters just ended up being a pretty awesome filter for our own style, and that’s wild for a franchise known for the opposite.
